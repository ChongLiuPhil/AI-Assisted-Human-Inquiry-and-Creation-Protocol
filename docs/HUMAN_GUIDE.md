# Start Here: A Human Guide to Inquiry, Creation, and AI Assistance

> This is the **human first entry point** to the Inquiry Publishing Stack.  
> You can start here even if you do not know what GitHub, agents, YAML, or Cloudflare are.  
> You do not need to learn the tools first. Start by understanding why the system exists, how it can help you, and which decisions should remain yours.

---

## 1. Understand the system in one minute

Every day you may:

- want to learn something new;
- become curious about a question;
- need to complete an assignment, article, report, research project, or book;
- collect so much information that it becomes hard to tell what matters;
- ask AI many questions and later lose the evidence behind an answer;
- switch AI tools and have to explain everything again;
- receive polished AI writing without knowing whether it is correct;
- keep work private now but possibly publish it later.

This system is not designed to make AI think instead of you.

It is designed to answer a different question:

> **How can you remain in control of your questions, evidence, judgments, decisions, and work while AI becomes an assistant that can help you, be inspected, and be replaced?**

In the simplest form:

~~~text
You ask questions, choose direction, and make important judgments
        ↓
AHICP keeps the thinking process clear
        ↓
Your project preserves sources, ideas, decisions, and work
        ↓
PPF helps organize and publish the work reliably
Vault Interface exposes only information that should be public
Starter helps you and an AI configure the system correctly
~~~

You do not need to learn all of this at once.

## 2. Why ordinary chat is not enough

Chat is convenient, but long-running work kept only in chat tends to fail in predictable ways.

### 2.1 You forget why

You may remember the final conclusion but forget:

- which sources you used;
- which statements were guesses;
- why you rejected another option;
- which decision you personally approved;
- which sentence was only an AI suggestion.

### 2.2 AI can also lose context

Different chats, models, and agents may have different context.

If a project exists only inside one conversation, changing tools often means explaining everything again.

### 2.3 A polished answer is not automatically a reliable answer

AI can generate fluent text.

Fluency does not automatically mean that the text:

- has evidence;
- is complete;
- separates speculation from fact;
- represents what you actually mean.

### 2.4 The longer the project, the easier it is for confusion to accumulate

A one-day question may fit inside chat.

A project that lasts months or years needs durable places for:

- the current goal;
- confirmed facts;
- important sources;
- approved decisions;
- unresolved questions;
- next steps;
- constraints that should not be changed casually.

AHICP turns these important states from temporary conversation into inspectable project state.

## 3. Habits the system is designed to strengthen

The purpose is not only speed.

It is also to strengthen reliable habits of inquiry.

### 3.1 Clarify the question before rushing to an answer

Instead of immediately asking:

> "Give me the answer."

first ask:

- What exactly do I want to know?
- Why does it matter?
- What would make an answer useful?
- What constraints apply?

### 3.2 Separate facts from your judgment

For example:

> "This source says A."

and:

> "Therefore I believe B."

are different statements.

The first can be checked against a source. The second requires reasoning.

### 3.3 Preserve uncertainty

Not knowing is not failure.

A reliable project can clearly record:

- known;
- unknown;
- disputed;
- still needs verification.

### 3.4 Preserve the reason behind important decisions

Then your future self, another collaborator, or another AI can answer:

> "Why did we do this?"

### 3.5 An AI proposal is not your commitment

AI can propose.

A proposal becomes an adopted project decision only after the appropriate human approval.

### 3.6 End work in a state that can be resumed

A good project state lets you return months later and still understand:

- where you stopped;
- why the project is in its current state;
- what should happen next.

## 4. What each of the four components does for you

The four projects are logically independent, but they can form one complete working system.

### 4.1 AHICP: keep control of thinking and decisions

**AHICP — AI-Assisted Human Inquiry and Creation Protocol**

It addresses the question:

> How do I think, research, and create with AI without giving my judgment to AI?

It helps preserve:

- goals;
- questions;
- sources;
- evidence;
- reasoning;
- hypotheses;
- decisions;
- human approvals;
- current work state;
- handoff information.

AHICP is therefore the **human-method center** of the ecosystem.

[AHICP homepage](https://chongliuphil.github.io/AI-Assisted-Human-Inquiry-and-Creation-Protocol/)

### 4.2 PPF: keep work durable, buildable, and publishable

**PPF — Personal Publishing Framework**

It addresses:

> How can my article, research, book, course, or knowledge project remain rebuildable without depending on one website?

It focuses on:

- canonical source;
- web output;
- book/document output;
- builds;
- publication state;
- releases;
- Continuous Web;
- rebuildability.

[PPF homepage](https://chongliuphil.github.io/Personal-Publishing-Framework/)

### 4.3 Vault Interface: expose only what should be public

It addresses:

> How can a project be understood publicly without exposing private work?

It provides a public metadata contract.

A project may publish:

- name;
- summary;
- public site;
- publication state;

without publishing:

- private research notes;
- working memory;
- unpublished manuscripts;
- passwords;
- private repository locators.

[Vault Interface homepage](https://chongliuphil.github.io/Vault-interface/)

### 4.4 Starter: compose the pieces correctly

Starter addresses:

> I do not want to research every configuration detail. Can an AI set this up correctly for me?

It handles:

- composition;
- profiles;
- adoption;
- upgrades;
- machine-readable state;
- agent configuration entrypoints.

For a complete new project, the current default baseline is:

~~~text
full AHICP
+ full PPF
+ Vault Interface
+ project-owned content
~~~

[Starter homepage](https://chongliuphil.github.io/Inquiry-Publishing-Project-Starter/)

## 5. What changes before and after adoption

### Temporary chat only

~~~text
Have a question
→ ask AI
→ receive an answer
→ ask another question
→ copy some text
→ weeks later, lose the evidence
→ cannot tell what was confirmed
→ switch AI and explain everything again
~~~

### With this system

~~~text
Ask a question
→ preserve the goal
→ collect and retain sources
→ AI helps organize and analyze
→ separate facts / inferences / suggestions
→ human confirms important decisions
→ preserve current work state
→ produce the work
→ publish according to authorization
→ another human or AI can continue
~~~

The key difference is not "more files."

It is:

> **The project no longer depends on the memory of one conversation.**

## 6. What AI can do, and what it should not own

### AI is well suited to help with

- finding and organizing sources;
- comparing views;
- making tables;
- summarizing long material;
- detecting inconsistencies;
- proposing structure;
- drafting;
- checking citations;
- repetitive repository maintenance;
- building websites;
- executing technical steps under approved rules;
- preparing handoff material.

### Humans should retain responsibility for

- why the project exists;
- which questions matter;
- which values matter;
- whether to accept an important interpretation;
- whether to adopt a key proposal;
- which private material may become public;
- whether to publish;
- what responsibility to take for the final result.

In one sentence:

> **AI can expand your capability without becoming the owner of the project.**

## 7. Three simple examples

### Example 1: a student makes a dinosaur project

Without the system:

- ask several AI systems;
- copy answers;
- lose track of sources;
- struggle to explain why the information should be trusted.

With the system:

1. Record the question: "Why did the dinosaurs go extinct?"
2. Preserve several reliable sources.
3. Ask AI to separate established evidence from disputed explanations.
4. Choose the focus of the report.
5. Keep the sources.
6. Finish the work.
7. If publishing a website, publish the final work without exposing private drafts.

The student does not need to know Git. An AI agent can maintain the structure.

### Example 2: long-term learning

Imagine learning economics, history, programming, philosophy, or a language over six months.

The hardest problem may eventually become:

- forgetting what you already learned;
- losing unresolved questions;
- giving a new AI no context about your path.

AHICP can preserve the learning path, sources, changes in understanding, and current questions.

### Example 3: writing a book or conducting long research

All four components can participate:

~~~text
AHICP
→ preserve research purpose, argument, evidence, and decisions

PPF
→ manage manuscript, builds, and publication

Vault Interface
→ expose safe public project metadata

Starter
→ let an agent compose and upgrade the components correctly
~~~

## 8. Privacy and publication are different decisions

This is a central principle.

You can have:

~~~text
GitHub source repository = private
Web publication = restricted
~~~

and later:

~~~text
GitHub source repository = private
Web publication = public
~~~

Therefore:

> **Publishing the final work does not require publishing the entire private working process.**

Original, unpublished, or private material stays private by default.

Only approved material enters public output.

Passwords, tokens, private keys, and other secrets should never be committed to repositories.

## 9. What if I know nothing about the technical tools?

That is fine.

You do not need to first learn:

- Git;
- GitHub branches;
- YAML;
- CI;
- Cloudflare;
- APIs;
- command-line tools.

You can begin with ordinary language:

> "I want to start a long-term project. Please set it up according to AHICP / the Inquiry Publishing Stack. Do as much of the technical configuration as you can, and only ask me when I need to decide, approve, sign in, or enter a secret. Give me clear step-by-step instructions when I need to act."

An agent following this ecosystem should:

1. understand the project;
2. default to full AHICP + PPF + Vault Interface;
3. protect original and unpublished material by default;
4. create required project files;
5. run validation;
6. automate technical work where possible;
7. provide precise steps when human action is truly necessary;
8. never ask you to paste secrets into chat;
9. report the resulting project state.

If the agent cannot operate a browser, browser work can be handed to a browser-capable agent or Work mode.

## 10. First use: do only five things

### Step 1: tell the AI what you want to do

No technical vocabulary is required.

For example:

> "I want to study urban transportation over several months and eventually write an article."

### Step 2: tell the AI what it must not decide alone

For example:

> "Ask me before public publication."

### Step 3: ask the agent to use the complete default stack

For example:

> "Set this project up using the complete default Inquiry Publishing Stack. Preserve my goals, sources, decisions, and work state. Keep original content private by default."

### Step 4: work normally

You can still say ordinary things:

- "Help me find sources."
- "Compare these two explanations."
- "I do not understand this."
- "Record the decision I just made."
- "What should I do next?"

### Step 5: make the project remember the state

At the end of important work, say:

> "Write confirmed decisions, unresolved questions, and next steps back into project state so another AI can continue."

## 11. Learn gradually instead of all at once

### Level 0 — only use conversation

You express goals.

AI maintains structure.

### Level 1 — read project state

You learn where to find:

- the current goal;
- sources;
- approved decisions.

### Level 2 — inspect evidence and changes

You can check:

- why a decision was made;
- which files changed;
- whether AI followed the rules.

### Level 3 — control publication

You understand:

- private source;
- restricted Web;
- public release;
- publication authorization.

### Level 4 — manage complex systems

Only here do Git, CI, Cloudflare, and agent routing become useful topics to study deeply.

The purpose is not to force everyone to become an engineer.

The purpose is:

> **Let the system and agents absorb technical complexity while humans retain key judgment.**

- create a project;

- configure a profile;

Therefore:

~~~text
Human conceptual entry
        ↓
      AHICP

Machine/configuration entry
        ↓
      Starter
~~~

The two entrypoints are connected, but they have different responsibilities.

## 13. What the system does not promise

It cannot guarantee that:

- AI is always correct;
- every source is reliable;
- every project succeeds;
- more records automatically make you smarter;
- process can replace real learning;
- automation can replace human responsibility.

It provides a structure that makes it easier to detect errors, preserve evidence, continue work, and clarify responsibility.

## 14. Ten ideas worth remembering

1. **The question belongs to you, not the AI.**
2. **An AI suggestion is not your decision.**
3. **Separate facts, inferences, and opinions.**
4. **Important sources should remain checkable.**
5. **Important decisions should preserve the reason.**
6. **It is acceptable to record that something is unknown.**
7. **Chat can be temporary; project state should be durable.**
8. **Tools can change without losing human purpose.**
9. **Publishing a work does not require publishing the whole private process.**
10. **Let AI absorb technical complexity while humans retain key judgment.**

## 15. Start for real: send one instruction to your AI

At this point, you do not need to learn GitHub, Starter, YAML, Cloudflare, or deployment terminology first.

Starting for real takes only four steps:

1. **Copy the bootstrap instruction below.**
2. **Open the AI you normally use, start a new conversation, and paste it.**
3. **Add one sentence describing what you actually want to do.** For example: “I want to study urban transportation over the long term and eventually write a book.”
4. **Send it.** The AI should then enter the machine entrypoint, retrieve the technical contracts, create or inspect the project, and begin the engineering setup.

Bootstrap instruction to give the AI:

~~~text
I want to start using AHICP / the Inquiry Publishing Stack.

First read and follow the machine entrypoint:
https://chongliuphil.github.io/Inquiry-Publishing-Project-Starter/agent/

You must retrieve the ecosystem, Agent Retrieval Contract, and required upstream specifications linked from that entry, then work from the complete default stack: full AHICP + full PPF + Vault Interface, with original and unpublished source private by default and unpublished Web restricted/authenticated by default.

First confirm that you successfully loaded the machine entrypoint, then use the project goal I provide next to create or inspect the project.

Complete routine technical configuration, repository changes, validation, deployment preparation, and durable state write-back yourself when tools permit. Ask me to act only when key judgment, approval, sign-in/MFA, authorization, or direct secret entry is truly required, and then give precise numbered steps, completion criteria, verification, and rollback.

Never ask me to paste passwords, API tokens, private keys, recovery codes, or other secrets into chat.
~~~

Then add one sentence at the end of the same message:

> **What I want to do is: …**

After you send it, the AI should normally tell you that it loaded the machine entrypoint, confirm your project goal, and continue the engineering setup.

You do not need to open the machine entry yourself or read the technical GitHub files. Those are for the AI and for people who want to inspect the implementation in depth.

## Final principle

Tools, models, and platforms will change.

What should endure in a long-running project is:

- human purpose;
- inspectable evidence;
- clear decisions;
- resumable work state;
- control over privacy and publication;
- human responsibility for the final result.

That is what AHICP and the broader Inquiry Publishing Stack are designed to protect.

**Once you understand the system, the next step is not to study more tools. Send the bootstrap instruction to your AI and tell it what you actually want to do.**
